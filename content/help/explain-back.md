# Explain-back

## What explain-back is
Explain-back is the only mastery check in LearnProof. There is no quiz. After you work through a stage's resources, you explain the stage's ideas in your own words, one idea at a time. A stage is recorded only after every idea has passed.

## How an idea passes
Each idea is sent to the review model on its own. An idea holds when you explain what it is, and why it matters or when you would use it, in your own words. Naming the idea, a single clause, or a wrong or confused explanation does not pass. Every answer gets a written review back, whether it holds or not.

## Speaking instead of typing
Press Speak instead above the answer box and explain the idea out loud. Your browser turns your voice into text and adds it to the box as you talk, so you can read it, fix a word, or add to it before you press Check my explanation. Press Stop listening when you are done. Spoken answers are judged exactly like typed ones. Speaking works in Chrome, Edge, and Safari; the microphone needs your permission the first time. LearnProof never receives or stores your audio, only the words in the box.

## Your own words, not an AI tool's
Explain-back only works if the words are yours. Each answer is checked for signs that an AI chat tool wrote it or that it was pasted in: chatbot phrases such as "I hope this helps", headings and bullet lists, stock essay phrases, most of the answer arriving by paste, or text arriving faster than anyone types. The review model gives its opinion too, but its opinion alone never flags an answer, and casual, short, imperfect, or second-language English is fine. A flagged answer still counts: it is reviewed like any other, and if it explains the idea it passes and you move on. Each flagged answer shows a warning and takes 5 points off your Retention score on Progress, up to 40 points in all. Only a count of flagged answers is kept, never the flagged text.

## When an answer is thin
An empty answer stays on the step. A wrong or thin answer also stays on the step and shows a written review that says what is missing. You can try that idea again as many times as you like. Nothing lets you skip an idea.

## What happens when every idea holds
Each idea that holds is saved to Notes with your explanation and its review. When the last idea holds, the stage is recorded as passed and the next stage on the path opens, unless the path is a practical one.

## Hands-on work on practical paths
On paths where the skill is something you make or perform (Full-Stack Web Development, IoT and Robot Automation, Music Production, Animation, Graphic Design, Photography, Art and Painting, Guitar, Badminton, Sound Design, Content Creation, Travel Vlogging, Podcasting, Cybersecurity, Interior Design, Public Speaking, Chess, Screenwriting, SEO, Digital Marketing, AI Tools, and Freelancing), explaining is half of it. After the last idea holds, the stage asks you to show one small piece of real work that uses it: a link, a photo or screenshot, a short video, or a PDF, whichever that path offers, and at least 40 words on what you did, how, and one thing you changed or fixed. The reviewer cannot see photos or videos, so it reads your write-up, and the text of a linked page such as a repository when it can. The write-up goes through the same own-words check as your answers. Once the work is accepted, the stage is recorded. Paths about knowledge or personal life, such as Psychology, Nutrition, or Personal Finance, stay explain-back only. Stages you passed before this was added stay passed.

## Where the ideas come from
The ideas are written from the stage's own resources the first time the gate opens, and again when those resources change. Every key point from the resources is kept. Explain-back runs in focus mode, which hides the sidebar, and your draft is kept in this browser until the stage is saved.

## When the review cannot run
If the model cannot be reached, times out, or replies with something that is not a review, nothing is saved and you can try again.
